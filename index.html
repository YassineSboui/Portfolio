01

Multi-Tenant Platform · NeoLedge

Elise.Studio

Nine business modules behind one console - eight mounted in the shell, the ninth a control-plane API - for enterprise customers who used to buy several of these as separate applications, NeoForm among them. The forms module is where NeoForm went next: the same builder carried onto .NET 10 inside a nine-module console, with the tenant boundary hardened from one check to four, the runtime script guard made configurable and an egress guard added around the connectors. Build a form by dragging fields onto a canvas and publish it to a link a stranger can open without an account. Draw a workflow as a graph and get every run traced back step by step with real timings. Compose dashboards whose aggregation is pushed down into PostgreSQL instead of being assembled in the API, read through a role that cannot bypass row-level security. Wire connectors to REST, SQL, storage and e-signature services with credentials held in a vault and never readable again. Underneath, every request's tenant is enforced four separate times, down to row-level security in PostgreSQL, and a conformance test goes red the moment a table ships without a policy.

Decisions

  1. 01SSRF filter

    The constraint Connectors have to reach customer systems — REST, SQL, storage and e-signature services we do not control.

    What I rejected Validating the URL.

    What I did A filter in the socket connect callback, refusing private-network targets on resolved IPs before any socket opens — closing the DNS-rebinding window.

    What it cost The check runs per connection, not once, and DISCOVERY_EGRESS_BLOCKED is a refusal a customer will occasionally see legitimately.

  2. 02Publishing 0.524

    The constraint The triage classifier's held-out accuracy is 0.894.

    What I rejected Shipping 0.894 alone.

    What I did Published 0.524 cross-source transfer recall beside it, in four places, because holding out an entire CDC/WHO source passage shows the classifier is largely learning one page's phrasing.

    What it cost Cholera reads 0.015, in public.

  3. 03The 62 cap

    The constraint Keyword ATS filters rank a job title above a candidate who can do the work, and explain neither decision.

    What I rejected One confidence score.

    What I did Two stages of ceilings cap matches supported only by the job title: a title hit with a skills sub-score under 35 is held at 62.

    What it cost Real title matches get capped too.

  4. 04Discard the reply

    The constraint The assistant answers foreign patients for Turkish clinics over WhatsApp, and must never quote a price it invented.

    What I rejected Correcting the number.

    What I did Every price the model writes is re-extracted against an allow-list built from the clinic's price list and FAQ; a reply carrying an unlisted figure is discarded whole, booking marker included.

    What it cost The patient gets a fixed "our coordinator will confirm your plan first" line instead of the answer they asked for, and the reply the model did write is discarded with it, booking included.

  5. 05Nothing mounted

    The constraint 37 components, an 8-state field spec, seven of those states resolved by a six-branch priority chain — disabled > readonly > invalid > warning > focused > default.

    What I rejected Rendering components to assert it.

    What I did Passthrough classes computed as pure functions of props and context, asserted in unit tests with nothing mounted, including a test that forbids physical padding so RTL stays correct.

    What it cost The tests assert classes, not pixels.

  6. 06This page

    The constraint Seven demo videos of real running product, on one page.

    What I rejected Autoplay and eager loading.

    What I did preload="none", 1200x675 posters, one simultaneous load, and a manual-pause flag that scrolling never overrides.

    What it cost A deliberate delay before a video starts. Result: 113 MB to 24 MB, scroll p99 99.7ms to 17ms, simultaneous loads 8 to 1, and a video you pause stays paused.

Low-Code Platform · NeoLedge

NeoForm

NeoLedge's ECM product needed custom intake forms for every deployment — a developer's project each time. NeoForm turns that into configuration: admins compose forms, tables and referentials, wire runtime behaviour in Blockly, and publish them inside the authenticated Studio or as anonymous public links. I built across it — the canvas, its 38 component types and 45 validation rules, the Blockly layer that wires them and the AI co-designer that drafts them — and the security work is mine end to end: PostgreSQL row-level security as a second tenant boundary, an AST gate in front of the server-side JavaScript sandbox, and an SSRF filter that runs in the socket connect callback rather than at URL-validation time, closing the DNS-rebinding window.

Notes

  • PostgreSQL row-level security per tenant, proven by tests that bypass EF entirely
  • AI co-designer: the model emits typed operations, a deterministic compiler builds the Blockly logic
  • Deny-by-default authorization enforced by a test that walks the live endpoint table
  • Outbound connectors across four SQL engines behind a read-only statement gate; the REST connector pinned to a socket-level SSRF filter

03

Design System · NeoLedge

NeoLibrary

Every product team at NeoLedge restyled PrimeVue independently, so brand-critical field states drifted. NeoLibrary makes that one tested source of truth: 37 Vue 3 components - the 25 that wrap PrimeVue 4 render it unstyled under 27 per-component passthrough configs, where every state-dependent DOM node's classes are computed as pure functions of props and context — an 8-state field spec, seven of them resolved by a six-branch priority chain (disabled > readonly > invalid > warning > focused > default), asserted in unit tests with nothing mounted, including a test that forbids physical padding so RTL stays correct. It also ships NeoBlockly, a visual rule editor with 187 registered block types that compiles a workspace into async JavaScript.

  • RTL direction resolved through 5 ordered sources, enforced by class-level tests

04

Matching Engine · Personal

Talent Bridge

Keyword ATS filters rank a job title above a candidate who can do the work, and explain neither decision. Talent Bridge puts an uploaded PDF through a CNN document gate that accepts it, rejects it, or holds it back as unverified — the stored sample accepts at 0.997 — grades its structure and content 96 out of 100 on a hybrid deep-learning-and-rules path, and scans its text against a fixed 84-skill vocabulary. That profile is then scored against job rows from a SQL Server star-schema warehouse on seven weighted components — skills 0.35, MiniLM embedding similarity 0.25, then role, experience, education, location, opportunity — and the API response carries every sub-score, matched and missing skills, and written reasons. Demand comes from the corpus itself: 668,704 of 785,741 scraped postings carry parsable skills. The hard part was false positives: two stages of ceilings cap matches supported only by the job title, so a title hit with a skills sub-score under 35 is held at 62.

▶ Demo

Notes

  • Two-stage cap layer ceilings title-only and low-signal-skill matches before ranking
  • Missing skills tiered critical to low by demand across 668,704 skill-bearing postings
  • SSIS star schema, 14 tables, bridge tables decomposing each posting's skill list
  • CNN plus text-evidence document gate, then a hybrid DL-and-rules quality grade out of 100

WhatsApp Assistant · Personal

Clinic AI

Turkish clinics sell to foreign patients over WhatsApp, where replies come late and without a price. This answers the Meta webhook in the patient's language and books against an editable weekly calendar. Every price the model writes is re-extracted against an allow-list built from the clinic's price list and FAQ; a reply carrying an unlisted figure is discarded whole, booking marker included. Seven classes of bad booking marker are rejected before an appointment exists, and a coordinator can seize a thread mid-generation — the in-flight reply is dropped at send time. Exercised end to end against a Meta test number: pilot-ready, not yet in production.

  • Operator dashboard with no front-end dependencies, no innerHTML, 8 XSS vectors asserted

06

Menswear Brand Site · Personal

MRAYAQ

A brand site and back office for a Tunisian menswear label, built instead of paying for Shopify. Vue 3.5 front end, .NET 8 minimal API over SQL Server: 16 endpoints, 9 behind JWT admin auth, layered into Domain/Application/Infrastructure folders so no endpoint lambda touches the DbContext. Order creation accepts only product IDs and quantities, pricing every line from the database. The identity is the harder half — PrimeVue's Aura preset re-skinned by hand into emerald and gold, over French copy hardcoded in the components, with English labels and two lines of Tunisian derja set in Arabic.

  • Aura re-skinned by hand — 8 --p-* token overrides, the rest bespoke; Cinzel and Noto Kufi Arabic

Medical NLP · Personal

Symptom Triage Console

Free-text symptom notes in; a triage suggestion for one of 14 conditions out, with medication reference and warnings. Held-out accuracy is 0.894 — and the interface publishes 0.524 cross-source transfer recall beside it, in four places, because holding out an entire CDC/WHO source passage shows the classifier is largely learning one page's phrasing. Cholera drops to 0.015. Every answer also names the engine that decided and reports that engine's own accuracy, not the best one's. Building the measure that exposed all this was the hard part.
